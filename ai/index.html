The Experience

How It Works

No dashboard, no ticket, no developer to wait on. You send a message; the change goes live; you get a reply.

1

You send it

"Add Friday's show to the site." "Mark the burger sold out." A photo with "put this on the menu." Text it from your phone, or message the bot on Telegram or Discord. On SMS you get "Got it — working on this now" right away, so the wait never feels like silence.

2

It does the work, in your project only

Claude opens your site's files or your Plateful menu, makes the change, and puts it live. It works inside a walled-off folder that holds only your things: it can't reach another client, and it can't reach anything of yours you haven't handed it. Photos you send are straightened, resized, and placed where they belong.

3

It checks, then replies

Before it tells you a change is live, it loads your live site to confirm. Then you get a plain-language reply: what changed and where to look. Not what you meant? "Undo that last change" puts it back.

Every change is saved in your site's history, so nothing is ever lost. Anything it shouldn't handle by itself — a domain change, a store, a login, anything that needs an account or money — it says so plainly and passes to me: you're told, and I get the request as a note.

Straight Answers

What it is, exactly

Every sentence on this page is meant to be literally true of the system as it runs today. Here are the ones people ask about.

Hours

Today the service runs on a machine in my office, not a data center. It's on during my working hours, Mountain time, and off overnight. A text sent while it's off waits and is handled when it comes back on (SMS and Telegram; a Discord message sent while it's off needs to be sent again). It moves to a server before the first monthly plan bills, and this paragraph changes when it does.

Speed

Texts are picked up within a minute. A typical change — new date, new wording, a photo — is made and confirmed live in a minute or two. Big jobs take longer. If one can't be finished, you're told rather than left waiting.

When something breaks

You get: "I ran into some difficulties making that change, so I haven't put it live. Taylor's been notified and is on it." I get what you asked, the actual error, and how long it ran, and I fix it. Same if you hit the daily cap on messages: the bot stops and I'm told.

Ownership

Your site is a GitHub repository under my account, hosted free on GitHub Pages. The day we part ways I transfer the repository to an account you control, and the site keeps running; you'd just change it another way. A restaurant's menu lives in its Plateful account, which is already yours.

Questions

Common Questions

Do I need to be technical?

No. If you can describe what you want in a text message, you can use everything I set up. The bot replies in everyday language: "the change is live, check the Shows page on your phone," never file names or commands. The technical part is my job.

What if the AI breaks something?

It can't break anything permanently. Every change is saved in your site's history before it goes live, any change can be undone by asking, and the bot doesn't say a change is live until it has loaded your live site and seen it. If a change fails partway, it tells you it hasn't put it live and I get the error.

Do I actually own it?

Yes. Your website, its code, your domain, your customer data — and on the self-serve tier, your AI subscription too. The code sits in a GitHub repository under my account while we work together and is transferred to yours when we stop. The one exception is stated, not hidden: the texting service runs on my systems and my AI account, so it ends when the engagement ends. You keep the site; you'd just change it another way.

Why a monthly fee and credits?

They pay for different things. The monthly is the obligation: someone is on the hook, the system is kept running and current, and there's a person who reads the errors. The credits are the AI's actual usage, at three times what it costs me, which is what makes a business of many small "one more thing" changes possible to serve. On the self-serve tier there are no credits because the AI runs on your account.

Which apps can I use?

SMS (photos included), Telegram, or Discord: message the bot directly, or I can point a channel in your Discord server or a Telegram group at your project so your whole team can use it. Only people you've authorized get a response; anyone else gets a polite "I don't recognize you" and I'm notified.

What will it refuse?

Two kinds of things. Anything outside its walls it simply can't do: it works in a folder holding only your site and menu, and any tool or file outside that is blocked, not just discouraged. And anything that's my job — a domain, a store, a login, a form that emails someone, payments — it declines in a sentence and forwards to me. It also won't publish things that are illegal, harmful, or misleading; the terms spell that out.

Is it available 24/7?

Not yet, and I'd rather tell you than have you find out. It runs on a machine in my office during my working hours, Mountain time. Texts sent while it's off wait and are handled when it's back. It moves to a proper server before the first monthly plan bills, and this answer changes then.

What happens to what I send?

Your message and any photos go to my system, where Claude makes the change and writes the reply. The message carriers (Twilio, Telegram, or Discord), Anthropic, GitHub, and, for photos it finds or generates, Pexels and Google each handle their piece under their own terms. Photos you send for the site become public when they go live. I don't sell or share any of it. Details in the privacy policy; messaging terms in the terms.

Who is Patch?

The bot's name. Patch is Claude running under rules I wrote, with your project's permissions and nothing more. A switchboard operator patches a call through; that's the job. I'm the human on the other end, and Patch will tell you so if you ask.

What's the difference between the two website tiers?

Who runs the AI. On Self-Serve, Claude is installed on your computer, on your account — you open it and ask, and there's nothing to meter. On the managed tier you install nothing: you text a number, my systems do the work, and the AI usage is paid in credits. Same site, same build, same undo. One is a tool you own outright; the other is a service I run for you.
