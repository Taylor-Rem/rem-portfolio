The ledger repair: from the support desk to one click

Taylor Remund · Kingsley Management, 2023 – present

A resident ledger whose payments were allocated to the wrong charges used to be repaired by hand, by the support team. I automated that repair twice: first as a desktop tool for the support desk, then as a feature of the platform itself. The second version removed a full-time manual workload from the department.

The problem

I joined Kingsley Management in 2023 on the support side, as its automation engineer. The company's portal marks a ledger whose allocations do not add up with a red star, and clearing red stars was daily manual work: open the ledger, allocate or unallocate payments and charges through the portal's forms, check whether the star is gone, try the next thing.

Version one: automate my own job

A PyQt5 desktop app in hand-written Python (about 2,300 lines in the public repository, built August 2023 to November 2024) that drives the portal with Selenium. The part that mattered most was the red-star bot (main_tools/robots.py):

  • It takes a list of ledgers, opens each one, and skips any that carries no red star.
  • It tries the cheapest repair first and checks the star after every step: allocate payments; if the star is still there, allocate charges; if it is still there, unallocate both and allocate them again. It stops the moment the ledger is clean.
  • Bounced payments have their own routine: remove the bounce and its late fees, unallocate everything, then allocate again.
  • A worker thread keeps the window responsive and lets a run be cancelled between ledgers.

The same app worked the support desk's ticket queue, added credits and ran one-off batches from a CSV. A reconciliation pipeline in Pandas cut manual reallocation from days to minutes, and I later moved its hot path to direct MySQL queries.

Version two: make it a feature

In November 2024 I moved to full-stack development on the platform (Laravel, Vue, MySQL), and the tool became the specification for the real thing:

  • A one-click repair that clears the allocations of a target month and re-processes its transactions in order, month by month, unwinding and re-applying bounced payments as it goes.
  • An allocation engine in three tiers (the additional-rent pool, then designated credits, then general payments) that supports security deposits, separate resident and mortgage ledgers, and bounced-payment reversal.
  • A summary table that precomputes ledger errors. A dirty flag marks a ledger for recalculation in the background, so a screen can show errors without running validation queries on every request.

What changed

The repair went from a person working through ledgers to a button, and a full-time manual workload in the support department is gone.
